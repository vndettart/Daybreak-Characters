White Fang is a cat with the pride of someone who's never expected to be caught if he falls. Devoted only to the select few he's deemed worth the effort, he protects them fiercely.

Once someone earns his loyalty, their safety becomes an extension of his own pride, and he doesn't forgive slights against them lightly. White Fang's devotion is one masked behind his dismissiveness — Fang doesn't warm to strangers and doesn't perform any usual expectations outside ones concerning one's honor.

Though an arrogant cat, he rarely voices it; his contempt never leaves his maw but he doesn't hide it. He's had little time with the guidance of those wiser than him, which forced him to be discerning to a fault. Words rarely move him, and when provoked he reacts with icy anger.

Living on the belief that the only honorable death is one met in battle, White Fang is little more than a ghost.

◆ Appearance

White Fang is lean-built and long-legged, his body shape being almost fox-like. His coat is pale, near-white, broken up by soft grey shading across his back, shoulders, and haunches. His ears are tufted, smoky grey deepening almost to charcoal at the tips. A small crescent mark sits between his eyes, easy to miss unless you're close. His tail is his biggest feature by far: thick and heavily plumed. Long fur builds into a loose ruff at his neck and shoulders. His eyes are a pale violet-grey. Paws are tipped in soft pink pads.

◆ History

Pre-Group History

White Fang's mother left before he was fully weaned. He doesn't remember much of her besides a blurry dark-pelted figure.

His father raised him alone from there. He was not an especially warm cat, but he was a good teacher — White Fang learned to hunt properly by six moons and learned to fight by eight.

White Fang's father was a constant presence in his life; he took him along for every hunt and had him watch from nearby in every territory dispute. Onyx was a formidable tom and White Fang wanted to be just like him. All the time spent together was the closest thing to affection to Fang's young mind, but all it truly had been was an investment on Onyx's part.

At ten moons, his father decided his investment was complete. He told him, plainly, that he was old enough now to manage on his own, and left within the same day to take up with a new mate somewhere else.

White Fang stayed in that empty den for two nights, waiting, until hunger drove him to move on. He remembers the sound of crickets those nights more than he remembers anything his father actually said to him.

The time between then and the celebration of his first year of life, he nearly starved to death. Leafbare was cruel.

Ages 10 moons to 14 were the darkest of his life. He drifted the fringes of a stretch of twoleg territory, sleeping in gaps between fences and foundations, fighting cats twice his size for scraps that were barely enough to keep him alive. Fang lost more fights than he won until losing stopped being an option. He killed his first bird alone that stretch, and his first cat not long after, in a dispute over nothing more than a few feet of shelter.

Sleep was hard to come by.

It was in that same territory he met the cat who'd become the closest thing he had to a friend, Monet. Through him, White Fang was introduced to a rogue group forming under a tomcat named Warden. The group ran the territory his father had abandoned.

The group back then held smaller territory and killed less than it does now, but "less violent" never meant merciful — it held ground because it had to, not because Warden wanted to prove anything yet. White Fang didn't join looking for a family or a cause. He joined because the alternative was either starving to death or being killed.

He made himself known quickly. Cats who underestimated him because of his size learned not to twice. Warden noticed him for the same reason everyone else eventually did, and within a few moons of joining, White Fang had been named his second.

White Fang's second year of life differs from his first in that it wasn't spent alone.

As Warden pushed the group's territory further past the fringes, more strays and rejects joined — cats running from something, cats who wanted to eat regularly and nothing more. Fang had little patience for most of them at first. He'd earned his place fighting for scraps and expected the same of anyone who wanted to stay.

It was Monet who kept pulling him back from writing newcomers off, insisting some of them were worth the trouble even when Fang couldn't see it yet.

He and Monet grew close. Monet was a cat who stood by his side in a life that hadn't given him many. They ran the same patrols, slept side by side, and Monet was the only one who could push on Fang's sharper edges without drawing blood for it. For a while, that was enough. It was the closest White Fang had come to trusting anyone since Onyx left him in that empty den.

It ended during a border push into land another group had already claimed. Warden wanted it and sent a small patrol to hold the line before the other side noticed — Monet and Fang among them.

They noticed anyway.

Monet went down and Fang got there seconds too late to change it.

He killed the cat responsible.

It didn't fix anything.

Fang didn't blame Warden — he couldn't bring himself to. Blaming him meant joining the group in the first place was a mistake, and so Fang made himself useful past the point anyone asked of him. He took the patrols no one wanted, settled disputes with his sharp tongue before they could turn into something larger.

Warden noticed. He noticed the way he noticed everything that could be used.

The group has grown since, and so has the appetite behind it.

Warden speaks less about survival now and more about what's owed to him, about who's still loyal and who's already thinking of slipping the leash. He trusts fewer cats by the moon. Fang is still one of the few he does.

White Fang is more of a loyal hound than a clever fox — ready at a moment's notice to follow Warden's every command.
